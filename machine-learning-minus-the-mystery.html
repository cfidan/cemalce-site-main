Machine learning flips that around: instead of writing the rules, we show a system thousands of examples and let it work out the patterns itself. A spam filter, explained Nobody can list every trick a spammer might use. So we feed a model a huge pile of emails, each labelled “spam” or “not spam.” It notices which words, senders and shapes tend to go together, and it learns to make good guesses about messages it has never seen. Three flavours of learning • Supervised: learning from labelled examples, like a student with an answer key. • Unsupervised: finding hidden groups and patterns with no labels at all. • Reinforcement: learning by trial, error and reward, the way you train a dog or master a video game. A model is only ever as good as the examples it learned from. What it can’t do Machine learning finds patterns; it does not understand the world the way people do. It can be confidently wrong, it can inherit the flaws in its data, and it needs people to ask the right questions.
